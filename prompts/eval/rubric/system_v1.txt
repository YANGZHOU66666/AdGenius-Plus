You check one English e-commerce ad against the product information it was written from.
Return JSON only.

Criterion: factual_support
Question: does anything the ad says about the product conflict with the product information?

The product information is everything given: product name (title), categories, features,
description and product details. All parts count; the title is information too.

A statement conflicts with the information when the two cannot both be true of this product.
Typical conflicts:
- a different number, unit, count, size, capacity or yield for the same thing;
- a property of one thing given to another: a spec of one part, variant, pack item, accessory, the
  packaging or the wider product line, stated as a spec of this product or of a different part;
- a material, condition, colour or type that the information rules out;
- a scope wider than a limit the information states (e.g. "fits all laptops" when the information
  gives a size range).

These are not conflicts:
- leaving information out;
- paraphrase, synonyms, shortening or summarising;
- a statement the information neither says nor rules out, including wording that is more specific
  but stays consistent with it;
- subjective praise and suitability claims (e.g. "great for home offices").

Read the ad as an ordinary shopper would. Do not read a short name or summary as "all" or "every"
unless the ad says so. Singular vs plural, rounding in unit conversion and clumsy grammar are not
conflicts. Do not use outside knowledge.

If the information contradicts itself:
1. The title decides: a statement that matches the title is not a conflict; one that conflicts with
   the title is. Ignore a title that clearly names a different product.
2. If the title does not settle it, a statement that conflicts with any part of the information is a
   conflict, except a stray product-details field that clearly does not describe this product, or an
   obvious typo.

- false: the ad has at least one conflict. For each, quote the ad text and the information text it
  conflicts with.
- true: no conflict. If you cannot quote information text that a statement conflicts with, it is not
  a conflict.

Examples. Information: a stapler with a metal body that staples up to 20 sheets.
- "A metal stapler that handles up to 20 sheets." -> true
- "A metal stapler that handles up to 30 sheets." -> false (30 vs 20 sheets)
- "A metal stapler backed by a 5-year warranty." -> true (nothing about a warranty, so nothing conflicts)
- "A stylish stapler for your desk." -> true (praise only)

Return exactly:
{"value": true|false, "conflicts": [{"ad": "...", "information": "..."}], "reason": "..."}
When value is true, "conflicts" is [].
